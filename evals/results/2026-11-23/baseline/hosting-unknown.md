**Start by testing a small, customer-operated application with a locally served model on synthetic records.** This is a provisional route with low confidence in any specific product or model. You’ve identified sensitive records and strict infrastructure requirements. The two facts that would most change the recommendation are what users need to do with the records and which components must run on your infrastructure.

| Decision | Provisional choice and rationale | What would change it and what to validate |
|---|---|---|
| **Product surface** | Test a thin internal application if staff need AI inside an existing records workflow. An application gives you control over how users access records and where outputs go. | If the job is interactive Q&A or drafting, consider an approved chat surface. Confirm the workflow, who can access which records, and who may receive the output. |
| **Capability and model** | Leave the model choice open. For field extraction or classification, start with a simple baseline and a small locally served model that can produce the required output. For Q&A that depends on records beyond the supplied context, test retrieval that enforces record-level permissions and handles missing evidence. | Move to a more capable model if the simpler candidate fails on representative cases. Compare task accuracy, latency, and total operating cost. Confirm whether scans or images are required, since that changes the candidate set. |
| **Deployment and operations** | For the prototype, assume your environment hosts inference, record storage, and workflow execution. Confirm whether the boundary also covers any indexes, logs, telemetry, and backups. | If policy permits external inference for specific inputs, compare an approved managed API. Have security and infrastructure reviewers check the actual data flows, execution identity, retention settings, and hosting terms for the configuration under consideration. |

**Next step.** Have the workflow owner choose one representative task and propose pass criteria. Ask the security and infrastructure owners to document where each component and data flow may operate. Then have engineering test the simplest viable candidate on synthetic cases. Use the documented boundary and measured quality, latency, and operating cost to decide whether to proceed to a pilot.
