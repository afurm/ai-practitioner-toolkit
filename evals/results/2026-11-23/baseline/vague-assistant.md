The biggest gap is the workflow itself. A walkthrough can show whether the delay comes from drafting, finding information, or waiting for a decision. Each points to a different solution.

**30-minute call agenda**

1. **First 15 minutes — Walk through one recent case.** “Who starts the task, what happens from start to finish, and who uses the result? Can you show us a good finished example and where this case slowed down?” This identifies the first task worth evaluating and gives you a baseline.
2. **Next 8 minutes — Trace the inputs and actions.** “Which sources should the assistant rely on, who can see them, and what should the assistant do with the result?” This determines what information it needs and whether it should draft, send, or update a record.
3. **Final 7 minutes — Agree on what to validate.** “Which mistake would make the output unusable, who can judge that, and what improvement would make this worth pursuing?” This identifies a reviewer and gives the client a basis for setting a success threshold.

Ask the client to bring a redacted recent case and a good finished output, if they can share them. If they identify a suitable task, propose that the workflow owner confirm one representative synthetic or approved redacted case and name a reviewer. The first validation is complete when the reviewer compares an assistant draft with the current output, records material errors and review time, and decides whether a larger trial is warranted.
